## Cross-Cohort Transcriptomic Signatures of Osimertinib Resistance in EGFR-Mutant NSCLC Cell Lines

### Abstract (condensed)
Acquired resistance to osimertinib drives treatment failure in EGFR-mutant NSCLC. Using two public GEO cohorts of EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma cell lines — GSE202859 (discovery; parental vs chronically resistant “-OR”) and GSE193258 (validation; DMSO vs 3-week drug-tolerant persisters), restricted to the three shared lines PC9, H1975 and HCC827 (9 sensitive / 9 resistant each) — we derived a cross-dataset validated 1,112-gene transcriptional signature (significant and directionally consistent in both cohorts under DESeq2 `~ cell_line + condition`). The signature was characterised by GO/KEGG/Reactome over-representation and pre-ranked GSEA (cross-checked with R fgsea; NES Spearman rho 0.70–0.86). A pre-registered, leakage-controlled logistic regression (K = 50 features; group-aware nested cross-validation by cell line) generalised one-shot to the independent cohort (ROC-AUC 1.000, 95% CI 1.000–1.000; sensitivity 1.000, specificity 0.444 under the strict scheme). A composite stability/selection-frequency/SHAP score yielded a 100-gene candidate biomarker panel. Locked models recovered a drug-withdrawal gradient on an excluded time-course arm (transductive P(Resistant): DMSO 0.05 → DTP 0.80, decaying to 0.30 by 10-day washout), supporting partial biological reversibility. All findings are exploratory; no clinical or causal claims.

### Introduction (condensed)
Third-generation EGFR TKIs such as osimertinib improve outcomes but acquired, largely non-genetic resistance eventually emerges in nearly all patients. Single-cohort transcriptomic analyses are vulnerable to batch effects and optimistic performance estimates. This study therefore pairs an independent discovery and validation cohort with harmonised NCBI processing, background-controlled differential expression, pathway-level characterisation, and machine learning validated under leakage-controlled, pre-registered, one-shot external-validation discipline.

### Methods (condensed)
- **Cohorts.** GSE202859 (NovaSeq; parental vs -OR, de Miguel et al. 2023) and GSE193258 (HiSeq; DMSO vs 3-week 500 nM osi_DTP, Criscione et al. 2022); 18 samples each (9 S / 9 R); acute, shRNA and washout arms excluded from labels and tracked in coldata_all.csv.
- **Preprocessing/DE.** NCBI uniform counts (GRCh38.p13), gene-symbol mapping, min_count = 10, DESeq2 + VST, design `~ cell_line + condition`; significance at BH FDR < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1.
- **Signature.** Genes significant in both cohorts with consistent direction: 1,112 of 1,273 shared significant genes.
- **Enrichment.** ORA (per-source annotated universe) on GO-BP/KEGG/Reactome; pre-ranked GSEA primary, R fgsea exact cross-check.
- **Machine learning.** StratifiedGroupKFold nested CV (groups = cell line); feature selection and tuning inside folds; 4 model families × 6 feature-set sizes; pre-registered primary configuration logistic regression K = 50; discovery-fitted scaling (train_stats) as headline, transductive scaling (target_z) as optimistic bound only; external validation executed once after model lock.
- **Washout probe.** Blind VST refit on all 60 GSE193258 samples; locked discovery models applied to the excluded timecourse; label-free, interpretive only.
- **Reproducibility.** Fixed seed 42; end-to-end pipeline; decisions D1–D14 logged; leakage checklist enforced.

### Results (condensed)
1. **DEGs and signature.** Discovery: 2,785 significant genes (1,435 up / 1,350 down); validation: 4,852 (3,651 up / 1,201 down); 1,273 shared, of which 1,112 (87%) directionally consistent — a reproducible cross-study resistance signature.
2. **Biology.** Up-regulated: cell migration, extracellular-matrix organisation, cytoskeletal and developmental-lineage programs. Down-regulated whole-transcriptome programs (GSEA): Ribosome (NES −5.3 / −6.9), Spliceosome (−4.0 / −6.9), ribosome biogenesis, nucleocytoplasmic transport — a translation-suppressed, de-differentiated persister-like state. Python and fgsea engines concordant (NES rho 0.70–0.86).
3. **External validation.** Logistic regression, K = 50, strict scheme: ROC-AUC 1.000 (95% CI 1.000–1.000), sensitivity 1.000, specificity 0.444 (CI 0.111–0.778), accuracy 0.722, Brier 0.132; transductive bound fully separated (specificity 1.000, Brier 0.012). All 24 model-K cells reported with bootstrap CIs.
4. **Candidate panel.** Top composite-ranked genes include HECA, KLHL24, SACK1A, HEG1, IL33R, MAP3K3, TBC1D9, EFNB2, MIR198, MTURN; full 100-gene panel reported as candidates, not causal drivers.
5. **Reversibility.** Transductive P(Resistant) gradient: DMSO 0.05 → acute 0.68 → DTP 0.80 → washout decay (72 h 0.85 → 96 h 0.65 → 7 d 0.43 → 10 d 0.30) — partial, incomplete reversion, consistent with a state-like resistance phenotype.

### Discussion (condensed)
The consensus resistant state across two independently produced cohorts is one of increased motility/EMT/ECM/lineage plasticity and decreased translation/housekeeping activity — converging with the DTP literature and mSWI/SNF-mediated remodelling. Cross-study concordance, not the near-ceiling AUC, is the load-bearing evidence. Performance honesty is emphasised: bootstrap intervals, full result surface, strict-vs-transductive side-by-side, and pre-registered configuration selection. The specificity drop under the strict scheme reflects the documented -OR vs DTP endpoint mismatch (distribution shift), not overfitting. The washout gradient shows the signature is drug-exposure-sensitive and partially reversible, arguing against a pure exposure artefact. Limitations: n = 18 per cohort, three genetic backgrounds, no clinical context, conservative ORA universe, symbol aliasing requiring curation, and a single external validation run. Future work: patient-derived/organoid cohorts, single-cell resolution of the DTP trajectory, probability calibration, and multi-cohort panel ensembling.

### Conclusion (condensed)
A 1,112-gene cross-cohort signature of acquired osimertinib resistance, biologically characterised and tested under leakage-controlled pre-registered machine learning, discriminates resistant from sensitive states on an independent cohort with ROC-AUC 1.000 while honestly reporting sensitivity/specificity asymmetry. A 100-gene candidate biomarker panel and a label-free washout probe supporting partial reversibility complete an exploratory, conservatively stated resource for downstream validation — with no clinical, diagnostic or causal claims.
